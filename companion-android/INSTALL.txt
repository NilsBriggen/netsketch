NetSketch Survey — the companion app for Android phones (Android 10 or newer)

Install
  1. Download NetSketch-Survey-<version>.apk from the project's GitHub releases (or build it with
     ./build.sh) and copy it to your phone (USB cable, cloud drive, or e-mail it to yourself).
     New releases install over the old one and keep your points. A copy you build yourself is signed
     with your own key: to switch between that and a downloaded one, uninstall the old one first.
  2. Open it on the phone. Android asks to allow installing apps from this source — allow it once.
  3. Open "NetSketch Survey". The first measurement asks for permission to see nearby Wi-Fi networks
     (Android files this under location), and Location has to be switched on while you measure.
     "Watch my Wi-Fi" also asks to show a notification (Android 13+): it shows while watching and has a
     Stop button. Watching works without it, the notification is just hidden then.

Link it to NetSketch (recommended)
  1. On the computer: overview → Real measurements → "Link a phone (live)". A 4-digit code appears.
  2. On the phone: tap "Link to NetSketch" (top right). It finds the computer on your Wi-Fi by itself;
     if not, type the address NetSketch shows. Enter the code.
  3. Your floor plan appears on the phone. Pick the storey, tap where you're standing — the room name
     is filled in for you — and press "Measure here". The point appears in NetSketch right away,
     pinned exactly where you tapped.
  If the phone can't connect, the computer's firewall is blocking it: allow ports 47878 (TCP) and
  47879 (UDP). Points measured while not linked stay on the phone and are sent as soon as it's linked.

Without a computer nearby
  Measure anyway (it asks for the storey instead), then "Save & share file" and import the file in
  NetSketch under Real measurements → Import measurements…, and drag each pin to where you stood.

On the phone
  - The floor plan: pinch to zoom, drag to move once zoomed, double-tap to fit. Your points show as
    coloured dots (green = strong, red = weak); tap one to see what was measured there.
  - Each point lists what it shows in plain words (weak signal, a phone sticking to a far access point,
    the slow band, a crowded 2.4 GHz channel and which one to try), a channel chart per band, and every
    network heard. Long-press a point (or tap ⋮) to rename it, measure again at the same spot (the new
    measurement replaces the old point), or delete it. "Cancel" stops a measurement without adding a point.
  - The speed test measures download, upload, delay and its variation (about 35 MB), always over Wi-Fi.
  - "Quick point (signal only)" records just your own Wi-Fi's strength and speed in about 3 seconds,
    without a scan. "Ping test" checks how quickly and steadily your router answers.
  - Android lets an app scan for networks 4 times every 2 minutes. When that runs out the app waits and
    counts down (quick points don't count); turning off "Wi-Fi scan throttling" in Developer options
    makes surveys faster.
  - The app follows the phone's language (English or German) and its dark or light setting.

The other tabs
  - Around you: every network the phone hears, grouped into the boxes (router, extender, mesh node) that
    send them, with band, channel, width, Wi-Fi generation, security, a short signal history and what
    each one announces: fast roaming (802.11k/v/r), WPS, streams, country, and its distance where both the
    phone and the access point support Wi-Fi RTT (802.11mc). Yours are marked. It refreshes about every
    30 s while open (Android's scan limit).
  - Watch: "Watch my Wi-Fi" records every second (signal, access point, link speed, router delay) and scans
    when allowed, also with the screen locked. It lists dropouts, delay spikes, lost bursts, deep signal
    dips, switches between access points and any access point changing channel, with a timeline. The
    last 24 hours stay on the phone. It keeps the phone's processor awake: plug it in for long watches.
  - Check-up: plain-language findings from all of that and your measured points: crowded channels and
    the quietest one, routers that keep jumping channel (yours and the neighbours'), a phone sticking to
    a far access point, extenders whose link back is probably weak, settings (WPS, old encryption,
    40 MHz on 2.4 GHz, radar channels, separate names per band), and the internet side (double NAT or
    shared provider addresses, IPv6, slow name lookups). "Check my network" asks the router over UPnP and
    looks up your public address at Cloudflare.
  - Devices: what's on your network (UPnP and mDNS announcements, and a quick knock on common ports).
  - Send to NetSketch (or Save & share file) includes the watch log, the findings and the devices.

What a phone can't see (Android limits)
  - Which way an extender or mesh node really links back to the router: the app estimates it from where
    each one's signal is strongest, and says so.
  - Other devices' hardware (MAC) addresses (hidden from apps since Android 10), retry counters, or raw
    packets (that needs root).
  - The extra details of a scan (802.11k/v/r, WPS, streams, country) need Android 11; Wi-Fi 6 streams
    need Android 12. Distances need a phone with Wi-Fi RTT.

In NetSketch afterwards
  In the list of measured networks, networks named like yours are linked to your devices. For the
  others choose "Add as a neighbour" (or "Add … neighbours" for all): they get their measured
  channels, widths and busy time, placed where their signal fits your readings. Drag one to where it
  really is and press "Fit to measurements" to refine.

Build it yourself: ./build.sh (needs a JDK and the Android SDK; no Gradle).
